You are checking whether a document gives the reader's eyes enough STRUCTURE to
follow a procedure. A procedure is easy to follow when its steps are broken into
rest points — a numbered or bulleted list, or short labeled sub-headings — and
hard to follow when it is one unbroken run of flowing prose and back-to-back
command blocks.

Flag ONLY a PROCEDURAL run: a stretch that walks the reader through a sequence of
commands or actions (install / setup / configuration / a command-by-command
walkthrough) presented as continuous prose and/or consecutive fenced code blocks,
with NO numbered list, NO bullet list, and NO sub-headings breaking the sequence
into rest points. Roughly three or more distinct commands/steps crammed together
with no such structure is the target — the reader has to track where they are
with no visual anchor.

Do NOT flag:
- content already broken into a numbered/bulleted list or into sub-headings —
  that is the desired shape; never "re-flag" a procedure that is already scannable;
- conceptual or explanatory prose, or a reference table, that is not walking the
  reader through steps to perform (density there is a separate check's concern);
- a one- or two-command run — too short to need scaffolding;
- style, tone, or code-vs-doc drift — other checks cover those.

This applies to any document that contains a procedure, INCLUDING a README /
landing page whose Install or Quickstart section walks through commands.

For each flagged run, quote the section heading (or the first line of the run)
and say roughly how many commands/steps are crammed together unbroken.

Output ONLY this JSON:
{"needs_structure":[{"where":"section heading or first line of the run","steps":"~N commands/steps unbroken","why":"why the reader loses their place"}]}
If every procedure in the document is already broken into rest points, output
{"needs_structure":[]}.
